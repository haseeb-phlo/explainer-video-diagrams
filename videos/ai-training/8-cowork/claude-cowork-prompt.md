## Where it came from

This board **moved here from `videos/claude/11-cowork/`** (`build_cowork.py` ->
`claude-cowork.excalidraw`, module 2.11) and was **rewritten from nine feature beats to
nine argument beats** when the day-by-day series became the authoritative one - the same
move Day 4 made out of `videos/claude/2-projects/` and Day 7 out of
`videos/claude/4-skills/`. `videos/claude/` now has a third numbering gap, at 11. A gap
is not lost work.

It also **absorbs Dispatch from the retired day 13** - beat 8. That is day 13 of the
day-by-day series, **not** `videos/claude/13-chatgpt-to-claude`, which is an unrelated video
in the module-2 numbering and is untouched by this move.

The old module-2 board was a feature walkthrough: give it the whole job, it works on your
computer, goal in deliverable out, it uses your real material, you stay in charge, whole
tasks you'd hand it, who it's for, scope it tight, try one. Day 8 is a different video
with a different job - it teaches the *supervision model*, not the feature surface.
Everything displaced is on `claude-cowork-resource-card.md`; see "What came off the
board" below.

**Why this day exists:** the expensive rung. Chat is a conversation you supervise turn by
turn; Cowork is a shift you hand over, and the supervision model has to change with it.

**Ships:** one multi-step task handed to Cowork with its checkpoints named before it runs.
**Pause-and-try:** write the brief for a task you would hand over, then find the sentence
doing no work.

## How it is built

One flowing hand-drawn board, generated by `build_cowork.py`, which imports the shared
engine (`from excalidraw_kit import *`) and holds only the composition: the beat scaffold
(`OX` / `WID` / `ACCENT` / `beat_head`) and this board's bespoke illustrations. Never
hand-place elements in the `.excalidraw`; change the build script and regenerate. Never
edit `excalidraw_kit.py`.

```bash
python3 videos/ai-training/8-cowork/build_cowork.py     # rebuild this board
/usr/bin/python3 preview.py videos/ai-training/8-cowork/claude-cowork.excalidraw out.png
/usr/bin/python3 preview.py <scene> out.png XMIN XMAX   # close-up on one beat
python3 build_all.py                                    # rebuild all + Style-B guard
```

`preview.py` needs `/usr/bin/python3` - Pillow is on the system interpreter, not
Homebrew's. Beat *n* frames at `XMIN = (n-1)*2000 - 100`, `XMAX = (n-1)*2000 + 1250`.

`finish()` self-checks palette discipline, the hand font, absence of frames, text
overflow and text/text collisions before writing. The board builds with **397 elements
and 0 collisions**, and `build_all.py` prints **BUILD OK | GUARD PASS** with no other
board's output changed. `random.seed(80811)` keeps re-runs identical.

## Layout

Nine beats, one uniform slot each - content ~1120 x 980 (~1.15 : 1), `GAP = 800` of
whitespace between slots. You record by panning left to right and framing one beat at a
time on a 14" laptop; the whitespace is the camera. No two adjacent beats share an accent, so
every pan lands on a fresh colour. Beats 1 and 9 are both orange deliberately - they sit
sixteen thousand pixels apart and bookend the board.

| # | Accent | Heading on the board | What is drawn |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | orange | A conversation versus a shift | Left, a four-turn chat with a tick beside every turn. Right, one brief and a 3x3 grid of nine steps - the first three orange and watched, the last six grey. Caption: "you stop watching at step three". |
| 2 | violet | The brief is the whole job | Two rows. A thin brief whose steps wander off a dashed guide line and end in a document with a red cross; a specified brief whose steps stay on a solid line and end in one with a green tick. |
| 3 | blue | What a brief must name | Four numbered cards in a 2x2, each with a worked example line: the inputs, the output shape, what a gap means, when to stop and ask. |
| 4 | teal | Checkpoints, applied | The day 6 callback. Three panels, each reading source -> pause -> target: before the first write, at the join, before it leaves the folder. Manual/Auto/Skip named underneath. **Demo badge.** |
| 5 | indigo | It splits the work | Sub-agents. One brief fans into four workers and back through a join circle into one answer. Chip: "you review the join, not each branch". |
| 6 | red | Long runs hide their mistakes | The adversarial beat. Nine steps, the error at step 2, a dashed red bracket carrying it to step 9. What arrives: reads well (tick), adds up (tick), wrong since step two (cross). |

**Pan order:** left to right, beats 1 to 9, one slot at a time. Documented in the build
script's module docstring.

## Corrections made to this brief, and why

**Two factual claims in the brief were wrong against Anthropic's current documentation
and were corrected on the board. Do not let either back in.**

1. **Beat 7 - computer use is not a research preview.** The brief said "computer use, the
   screen-level version, is still a research preview - state it plainly and move on."
   Per *Let Claude use your computer in Cowork*, computer use is in **beta**, is **Pro
   and Max only**, and "Team and Enterprise plans don't have access to computer use at
   this time." Phlo is on a Team plan, so the accurate statement is more useful than the
   briefed one, not less: this audience cannot get it at all. The board says beta, Pro
   and Max only, not on Team or Enterprise.

2. **Beat 8 - Dispatch does not run in the cloud.** The brief said "the work continuing
   on the desktop or in the cloud as one conversation." Per *Assign tasks from anywhere
   in Claude Cowork*, Dispatch runs the task **locally on your desktop** - "your computer
   must be awake and the Claude Desktop app open while Claude works" - and it is a
   **limited beta on some Pro and Max plans**. The board draws the desktop only, and
   says both caveats in ink.

**The demo badge moved from beat 8 to beat 4, and this is a deliberate deviation.** The
brief put the only demo badge on beat 8: "dispatch a real multi-file task, then stop it
at the first checkpoint". Dispatch is Pro and Max only and Phlo is on Team, so that
cut-away is very likely un-recordable by the presenter. The pedagogical payload of the
briefed demo is *stopping a real multi-file run at its first checkpoint* - which is
recordable on Team, in Manual permission mode, and belongs on the checkpoints beat
anyway. The badge now reads "show in Claude desktop app: run a real multi-file task in
Manual, stop it at the first checkpoint", and beat 8 carries no badge, so the video never
promises a demo the presenter cannot shoot. **If Dispatch reaches Team**, add a second
badge to beat 8 rather than moving this one back.

Related, same shape: the built-in browser on beat 7 is an Owner-controlled toggle
(**Organization settings → Cowork → Built-in browser**, on by default on Team as it
rolls out). The board describes what it does and never promises it is switched on, the
same way the Day 3 memory beats say why you might not see it.

Minor: the brief calls this "the longest single-Loom board in the set". It is not - Day 4
is seventeen beats and about thirteen minutes. Nine beats at ~7 min 30 is stated instead.

## Decisions made while building, and why

**`STOPS` is the one place this board guesses, and it is pinned in one constant.** Beat 4
is a callback to the three checkpoints on the **day 6 autonomy-ladder board, which does
not exist in any worktree yet**. The three labels were derived from this brief's own
words ("the three stops from the ladder board placed against real file handoffs"):
*before the first write*, *at the join*, *before it leaves the folder*. They live in a
single `STOPS` list at the top of the build script, so reconciling with day 6 when it
lands is a one-line edit and not a re-layout. **Check them against day 6 before
recording.**

**Manual / Auto / Skip is the mechanism inside beat 4, not the three stops.** It is a
real, sourced product triad but it is a different axis - how much Claude asks, not where
it stops - so using it as the callback would have made beat 4 a re-teach of the product
rather than a callback to the ladder. It sits underneath the three stops as the setting
that implements them.

**Three colours are held constant across the board and are never beat accents.**
`RED` = a mistake or a thing you must not hand over (beats 2, 6, 7, 9). `GREEN` = a
checked, finished output (beats 2, 5, 6, 9) - beat 8's accent is green as an exception,
because Dispatch is the good-news beat and carries no green tick of its own. `GREY` =
the part of the run you have stopped watching (beats 1, 2). The tick/cross pairing is
the repo-wide convention.

**One worked example runs through beats 1, 2, 3 and 5** - reconciling two lists and
writing up the gaps. It is the brief in beat 1, the thin-versus-specified pair in beat 2,
the four things in beat 3, and the fan-out in beat 5. Figures are fictional and the
scenario is deliberately everyday business. Beat 6's nine steps are the same run seen
adversarially.

**The safety rule stayed on the board, on beat 9.** The old module-2 board had a whole
scoped-access beat (beat 8). The nine-beat argument has no room for it, but this is a
mandatory course for a regulated pharmacy and dropping the only on-screen data-handling
rule to a Resource Card would not survive clinical-safety review. It is now the second
anti-chip - *anything patient-facing without a gate* - with the gate spelled out
underneath: **Claude drafts, a person approves, then the record updates.** The
scoped-folder mechanics moved to the Resource Card and into the demo steps, where the
scoping is performed on camera rather than described.

**Beat 6 is the beat to protect if the video runs long.** It is the only beat that makes
the argument for checkpoints from the failure side rather than the instruction side, and
it is the one people remember. The droppable pair is beats 7 and 8.

**Pause-and-try and "Ships" are on beat 9 and in the script, not extra beats.** Nine
beats, as specified. The 10s hook / 30s why / pause-and-try / 15s close structure is a
shape for the narration and is carried in `claude-cowork-script.md`.

## Non-negotiables

- **Frameless.** No Excalidraw `frame` elements (a hard Style-B guard failure - it is the
  rejected Style A's signature). No boxes round beats, no connector spine, no inter-beat
  arrows, no colour-band washes, white background. "Separate frames for the video" means
  separate beat slots.
- All text in the Virgil hand font via the kit (`fontFamily: 1`), `roughness: 1`,
  `lineHeight: 1.4`. Headings via the kit's `heading()` - no step-number circle, no
  highlighter sweep behind a title, no decorative sparkles. Body and explanation text
  stays ink/grey; accents carry headings, labels, shapes and fills.
- Lively Excalidraw palette only, colour-coded per beat. Colour comes from vivid
  elements, not from background washes behind beats.
- Charming primitive illustrations over emoji. Light rotation jitter on notes and chips.
- Bespoke one-off illustrations (`folder`, `deliverable`, `monitor`, `step_box`, `phone`,
  `browser`, `bubble`) live in this build script, not in the kit. A kit edit re-styles
  every video and is a deliberate, rare act.
- **`chip()` labels must be single-line.** The kit sizes a chip's box for one line
  regardless of the label, so a `\n` in a chip label overflows the box. Beat 9's
  patient-facing anti-chip hit this and was flattened to one line.
- **Examples generic, not Phlo-specific.** Everyday business scenarios only - reconciling
  two lists, a folder of weekly notes, an invoices folder. No branding or logo, no
  patient or clinical content, fictional identifiers and figures only. Phlo-native
  material belongs in the live cut-away on beat 4, not on the board.
- Product proper nouns exact: Claude, Artifact, Project, Skill, MCP, Connector, Cowork,
  Claude Desktop, Claude Tag, Dispatch.
- British English, hyphens not em dashes, no Oxford commas.
- The live cut-away is marked with `demo_badge(...)` on beat 4 and with
  `[CUT TO CLAUDE DESKTOP]` / `[BACK TO BOARD]` in `claude-cowork-script.md`.
