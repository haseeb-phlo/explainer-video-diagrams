## The one idea underneath all eleven

The dangerous output is not the bad one. **It is the acceptable one.**

A bad output gets sent back. A tidy deck that argues nothing, a clean layout that says
nothing, a plausible figure where every cell checks out - those get shipped, because a review
checks whether something looks right, and they do look right.

So the move is always the same: **name what it has to get right, before you look at it.**

## Do one of these this week

**1 · Build one Project for the job you do most.**
Twenty minutes. Day 4 walks the whole thing. If you only do one, do this one - everything else
gets easier once the context stops being something you retype.

**2 · Turn your most-repeated explanation into one Skill.**
Then test it on a known input, where you already know the right answer, and see what it
quietly left out. That gap is the assumption you never said out loud.

**3 · Take something you already shipped and name the test it should have passed.**
Then run that test on it. This is the whole course in five minutes, and it is the one that
tends to change people's minds.

## What good looks like in ninety days

- You stop retyping context, because it lives in a Project.
- You name the test before you look at the output.
- "It looks fine" stops counting as a review.
- **And you still sign it off.** Claude drafts, a person approves, the record updates.

## The one rule that outlives the course

**No patient data. No confidential data. Nothing you would not put in an email to somebody
outside Phlo.** That holds whichever tool you are using - chat, a Project, a Skill, Cowork, or
any of the Microsoft 365 add-ins.

Two specifics worth remembering from the later days:

- **A file from outside can carry instructions you did not write.** Downloaded templates,
  vendor workbooks and data imports are the named risks. Review the confirmation prompts.
- **The Microsoft 365 add-ins do not inherit your organisation's data-retention settings, and
  their activity is not in Enterprise audit logs.** Where that matters, your own written check
  is the audit trail.
- **Outlook is the one where the output leaves the building.** Nothing sends itself, and
  nothing should leave until you have read what it commits you to.
