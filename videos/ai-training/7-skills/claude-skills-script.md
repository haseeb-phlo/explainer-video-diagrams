## 0:00 - 0:12 · Hook [CAM]

You have explained the same thing to Claude four times this month. Not four different
things - the same one, four times, because each new chat had never heard it. Today you
write it down once. It takes twenty minutes and you never type it again.

## 0:12 - 0:42 · Why this one matters [CAM]

Skills are the cheapest rung on this ladder and the first thing you will actually ship.
No coding, no admin request, no waiting for anyone. And there is an order to this: most
of the jobs people bring to Cowork later in the course should have been a Skill first.
Get this one right and the rest of the course gets easier, because you stop asking
Claude to work out the procedure every single time and start handing it the procedure.

## 0:42 - 1:22 · You have explained this four times [BOARD: beat 1]

Here is the pattern. Monday, you ask Claude for something and it comes back in American
English with bullet points everywhere, so you correct it. Tuesday, new chat, same
correction. Thursday, same. Friday, same.

Look at the four windows - the correction is identical every time. That is not Claude
being forgetful, and it is not something a better prompt fixes. It is a procedure living
in your head instead of in a file.

And there is the signal, bottom left: **the fourth time is the signal**. The first time
you type a correction, that is a conversation. The fourth time, it is a Skill you have
not written yet.

## 1:22 - 2:04 · Procedure, not context [BOARD: beat 2]

Now, the thing people get wrong. On the left, a Project - the tone guide, last quarter's
numbers, the team glossary. Reference material. Background Claude can look at.

On the right, a Skill - four numbered steps. Pull last month's figures. Check the
figures. Write it up in the house format. Flag anything over ten per cent. Not
background: a procedure Claude follows.

The line across the middle is the one to remember. **Context is what it knows, a Skill
is what it does.** If you find yourself pasting a document, you want a Project. If you
find yourself re-explaining the *order things happen in* - that is a Skill.

And they stack. The Project holds the background, the Skill runs the procedure, and they
work together perfectly well. This is not a choice between them.

## 2:04 - 2:46 · Search the gallery first [BOARD: beat 3]

Before you write anything, look on the shelf. Top row is already built in - Word, Excel,
PowerPoint, PDF. Bottom row is everything published by other teams, pushed out by your
admins, and brought in by partners.

The chip says it: **someone has already built this**. A Skill you enable in ten seconds
beats a Skill you spend an hour writing, every single time. Search first, write second.

And while we are on the subject of things other people can see - the red line at the
bottom, and this one is not optional. **A Skill is a shared file.** It can be published,
it can be pushed to a whole organisation, and somebody who is not you will open it.
Never put confidential or sensitive data inside one. Write the procedure, not the
particulars: no real names, no account numbers, nothing you would not put in a shared
folder. If your Skill needs sensitive input, the input goes in the chat at the time, not
in the file.

## 2:46 - 3:28 · The hidden assumption [BOARD: beat 4]

Right - you have searched, nobody has built it, you are writing your own. Here is where
almost every first Skill fails.

Step one, pull last month's figures. Step two, check the figures. And there is a hole
between them, and a colleague falling straight into it.

Because there is a step you have never written down. You have done this job forty times
and you always, automatically, exclude the trial accounts. It is so obvious to you that
it has never once reached the page. It is not in step one. It is not anywhere. And
Claude cannot infer it, any more than a new starter could.

So write it for someone who has never done the job. If a step assumes something, say the
something. And read it back as if you were new - which is exactly what the next beat is
for.

## 3:28 - 4:20 · Make it critique its own steps [BOARD: beat 5]

You cannot spot your own assumptions - that is what makes them assumptions. So get
Claude to do it.

Paste the Skill you wrote back in, and type the thing in the red box: *Here is a Skill I
wrote. Read it as if you had never done this job. Which step is ambiguous? What would a
new starter get wrong?*

And it comes back with a list. Step one says pull the figures - all of them? Step three
assumes I know what house format means. There is no example of a finished report. Three
holes you would not have found by rereading it yourself, because you already know the
answers.

Note what is happening here, along the bottom: **you are asking it to critique what you
wrote, not to write it for you.** You bring the procedure, Claude finds the gaps in it.
That way round.

`[CUT TO CLAUDE DESKTOP]` - demo badge: *show in Claude desktop app: ask Claude to find
the ambiguous step in your own Skill*

Let me do it on a real one of ours. Here is a Skill I wrote last week. Paste, same
prompt, and - there. It has found a step where I said "the usual format" and never said
what the usual format is. Thirty seconds, and I would never have caught that.

`[BACK TO BOARD]`

## 4:20 - 4:45 · Test on a known input [BOARD: beat 6]

Last thing, and it is the one people skip. Pick one input where you already know the
right answer. The March report - you know it comes to four thousand, one hundred and
eighty pounds.

Run it before the edit: five thousand and twenty. Wrong - version one counted the eight
hundred and forty pounds of trial accounts that you always exclude and never wrote down.
Run it after the edit, same input: four thousand, one hundred and eighty. Right, first
time, and every time after that.

Same input, both times. That is the whole test, and it is the only way you know your edit
actually worked rather than just felt better.

Then publish it, because - bottom left - **a Skill nobody else can run is a note to
self.** The point is that the next person does not have to learn what you learned.

## 4:45 - 5:00 · Pause here, and try it [CAM]

Pause the video. Go and find the correction you have typed most often this month. Not the
most important one - the most *repeated* one. Scroll back through your chats if you need
to. That correction is your first Skill, and it is already written; you just have not put
it in a file yet.

## 5:00 - 5:15 · Close [CAM]

What you are shipping today: one Skill, published to the gallery, tested on a known
input. That is the whole assignment. Context is what Claude knows, a Skill is what it
does - and the fourth time you type something is the signal to go and write one.

Resources and links are on the Resource Card below this video. Next up: Cowork - and you
will get more out of it now that you know which jobs should have been a Skill.
