## What you are shipping today

**One job off the board, run end to end.** Not a finished asset - one pass through the
six-step loop on something you actually needed this week.

**Pause-and-try:** pick that job, run the six steps, **then ask for the empty state**
before you send it anywhere.

## The one sentence to take away

**It will review its own work, find real problems and fix them - and that is still not a
second opinion.** It reviewed the screen it drew, using the data it invented.

## The six-step loop

Every job below runs the same six steps. Learn them once.

| | step | what it actually means |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | **prep the input** | gather the raw material first - the spec, the notes, the screenshots, the brand assets. More context in, less guessing out |
| 2 | **start simple** | two or three sentences, not a specification. Write it like a creative brief: goal, audience, constraints, format |
| 3 | **read the first draft** | all of it, before you touch anything |
| 4 | **refine wide, then narrow, then wide** | one whole-page pass, then one element, then one more whole-page pass |
| 5 | **ask it to review** | it audits its own work - readability, hierarchy, contrast, accessibility. **Then read the catch below** |
| 6 | **get it out** | export it, or hand the whole package over |

**Inside step 4, match the channel to the size of the change.** One element: comment on
the element itself. Structure or the whole page: say it in the chat. If a comment vanishes
before it is read - a known rough edge - paste the same feedback into the chat instead.

## The jobs this is actually good at

1. **A requirements document into clickable wireframes.** The spec nobody can picture
   becomes screens stakeholders can click through. It asks clarifying questions first -
   answer them properly.
2. **Prototyping live, inside a meeting.** Leave with the prototype rather than the notes.
3. **One-pagers and sell sheets** from a rough outline.
4. **Visual roadmaps** from a strategy document.
5. **Landing pages**, from references rather than from adjectives.
6. **Reusable social templates**, sized once and handed to whoever posts.
7. **Motion graphics for talking-head video** - see below, it is the surprising one.
8. **A tailored deck** - but that is Day 12 and the Claude PowerPoint video, not this one.

## Explore before you commit

Ask for one landing page and you get the layout this tool reaches for by default. That is
why so much output from it looks the same. The fix is a habit, not a setting:

- **Start from references** - two or three things you actually like. Not adjectives.
- **Ask for three directions, not one.** Comparing is faster than guessing.
- **Say what worked in each** - one's layout, another's colour, a third's use of type.
- **Only then build one**, out of the parts that earned their place.

One extra round, and the result is yours rather than generic. Related: **reference design
system components by name** if you know what they are called - it applies them more
accurately than a description.

## Motion graphics, the surprising one

Hand it the transcript of a video you have already recorded and it draws the graphics that
sit over the top of you talking. **The animation lands on the word you say it on** - that
is the whole trick, and it is why the transcript needs timings. Then you edit it in plain
English: slower, hold that number longer, drop the third one.

**Getting it out is the rough edge.** It plays in the browser, so you capture it off the
screen and drop that into your editor. **This is the single line on the board most likely
to change** - check it before you rely on it.

## The catch, written out

Beat 8 is the beat to practise, because the failure arrives looking like diligence.

**Two self-checks happen, and both are real:**

- **The one you never see.** It holds its output against your design system and corrects
  it before you look.
- **The one you ask for.** Readability, visual hierarchy, contrast, accessibility. A
  genuine audit, and worth running.

**Neither is a second opinion.** It reviewed the screen *it* drew, using the data *it*
invented: three rows, three short names, everything fits, because it chose what had to fit.

**So make it draw the states it never drew.** One sentence, falsifiable by looking:

> *"show me this with no requests at all, one that was refused and forty rows - one name
> sixty characters long."*

**Empty. Error or refusal. Real volume.** Ask for those three by name - they are the ones
it will not think of, and the only ones your team will actually hit. Anthropic's own
guidance says the same: check empty, error and loading states and different data volumes,
and **flag edge cases explicitly rather than relying on automatic detection**.

This is **Day 7's "test on a known input" and Day 10's "name the test before you look"**,
pointed at a screen. Same discipline, third object.

## Where it stops being the right tool

| the job | where it belongs |
|---|---|
| production-ready code | the handoff bundle, into Claude Code |
| pixel-perfect production design | your design tool - what comes out of here is a draft, not a spec |
| final polish, sizing and publishing | your brand tool |
| a full animated explainer video | a video editor. This makes graphics, not films |

Treat it as **exploration and a strong first draft**, then hand it on. That framing is the
second source's editorial judgement rather than Anthropic's, but it matches what the tool
is documented to do.

## The safety rules, repeated

**1. A shared design is a shared file.** Nothing confidential goes in - no private
codebase, no unapproved work, nothing patient-facing. Uploaded assets are stored and fall
under the same retention rules as everything else you put into Claude.

- Fictional placeholders only in any example: `Patient_001`, `J. Doe`, `NHS_TEST_123`,
  `acme.pharmacy@example.com`.
- Anything that touches prescriptions, dispensing or patient records keeps the human gate:
  **Claude drafts, a person approves, then the record is updated.**
- Check the Phlo AI Use Policy before anything made this way goes in front of a patient.

**2. Images.** It does not generate photographs - it leaves a correctly sized space.
**Fill it from Phlo's own asset library, or from a tool the AI Use Policy allows.** The
video deliberately names no third-party image tool; that call is the policy's.

**3. A design is never brand-final because Claude produced it.** A person does the last
pass and owns what ships. Carried over from Day 10 and Day 12 unchanged.

## The version-specific detail

Kept off the board on purpose - **all of it dates faster than anything on the board.**

### Access and plans

- **Where:** `claude.ai/design`, or the **Claude Desktop** sidebar. Web and desktop only.
- **Status:** a **beta** from **Anthropic Labs**, described at launch as a research
  preview. Newer and rougher than the rest of the tools in this series.
- **Plans:** **Pro, Max, Team and Enterprise.**
- **Phlo is on a Team plan, and it is default ON for Team.** It is **default off for
  Enterprise**. An admin controls it under **Organization settings > Capabilities**. So if
  a colleague cannot see it, that is an admin setting, not a plan limit.
- **Usage:** **no separate allowance** - it draws from the same pool as chat, Claude Code
  and Cowork. It used to have its own meter; that is history, and it is why you get much
  more use out of it than at launch. Heavy sessions still consume quota quickly.
- **Model:** a current top-tier Claude model, selectable in settings. **Do not quote a
  model number - check what is current.** This is the fastest-moving fact on the card.
- **Speed:** simple pieces render quickly; a large deck can take on the order of ten
  minutes. Plan around it rather than sitting watching.

### Where a design can start

- A text prompt, typed or dictated.
- Uploaded images and documents - **DOCX, PPTX, XLSX** - plus PDFs and screenshots.
- Your codebase, or a design-system repository.
- A web capture tool that grabs elements from a live site.

### The design system (Day 12 teaches this - here is the product surface)

- Built at **organisation** level from what you upload: a codebase or component library,
  prototypes and screenshots, decks or PDFs, or individual assets - logo, colour palette,
  type. **One source is enough; more gives it more to work with.** A design-file
  integration and a repository link are both supported.
- What it generates: colour palette, typography, components (buttons, cards, navigation)
  and layout patterns, plus sample screens to review.
- **Publish** it to make it the default for new projects. **Remix** opens a chat to change
  it. On Enterprise a dedicated admin permission controls who can publish, set the default
  and delete.
- **`/design-sync`** in Claude Code pulls a design system in; **`/design`** creates and
  edits design projects from the terminal.
- **Do not point it at a real Phlo codebase or at confidential design files** - on camera
  or otherwise - without approval.

### Collaboration and getting it out

- **Real-time collaboration** with comments, the way a shared document works.
- **Exports:** PPTX, PDF, standalone HTML, and a .zip of the project.
- **Handoff to code:** it bundles the design files, the conversation and a README, and
  gives you a prompt to paste into Claude Code.
- **Connectors** to a range of design, publishing and deployment tools, with more listed
  as coming. **Some integrations need to be arranged for the organisation** - check before
  promising one.
- **Sharing:** an internal link with **view**, **comment** or **edit** access.

### Known rough edges (documented)

- Inline comments occasionally fail to persist - paste the feedback into chat instead.
- Linking very large repositories causes lag and browser instability. Link a specific
  directory rather than a monorepo, and exclude `.git` and `node_modules`.
- Multi-person editing is unreliable.
- Design-system quality depends on the quality of what you gave it.
- No data residency support and no audit logging at time of writing.

## What we still could not verify

Two claims from the video transcript appear in **no** Anthropic page and are **not**
mentioned in the second source either. They are **not on the board and not in the
narration**, on purpose - a board watched by all staff must not be falsifiable on camera.

| Claim | Status |
|---|---|
| 3D objects can be downloaded as a printable 3D file | **Unverified.** The announcement mentions 3D only inside "code-powered prototypes with voice, video, shaders, 3D", with no download format named |
| A gallery of named templates on the home screen | **Partly.** Documentation describes org-level product and slide-deck templates *you* create, and prompting for what you want. A public template gallery is not documented |

**One earlier claim is now resolved as false.** The transcript showed generated animations
being downloaded as an MP4. The second source states plainly that there is no native
export and that screen recording is the workaround. **There is no MP4 download button** -
which is exactly what the board says.
