## Why this video exists

**Claude is excellent at the surface in both apps - fluent prose, tidy slides - and the
surface is exactly what a reviewer checks.** The substance is the part it skips unless you
ask for it. In Word that means a tightened paragraph that has quietly lost a condition; in
PowerPoint it means a deck that is well made and argues nothing. Same failure, two
artefacts, and that is why these are one video rather than two.

**Ships:** one real document edited with every change reviewed as a diff before acceptance,
one deck built from an agreed outline with a headline on every slide that states a
position, and - since 2026-09-17, when the Outlook add-in Loom was attached to this Day -
one drafted reply read for what it commits to before sending.
**Pause-and-try:** take a paragraph you wrote this week and ask for a change rather than a
rewrite - or take your last deck and rewrite three slide titles so each states a claim - or
take a reply you sent this week and read it for what it promises, not how it reads.

**Why the third strand belongs here and is not padding.** Day 14's thesis is that Claude is
best at the surface and the surface is exactly what a reviewer checks. The Outlook failure
is the same failure a third time: a warm, well-pitched reply that passes review on tone and
quietly agrees to a date, a price or a liability. Adding the reply to the task makes the
Day's one argument land on all three artefacts it now covers.

## The merge, and what it cost

The two source briefs are 7 beats each. That is ~10 minutes of narration and cannot be one
video. Four merges bought the room:

**1. The adversarial beats fused, and that fusion is the whole idea.** Word's "the rewrite
that drops a clause" and PowerPoint's "the plausible deck" are **the same failure** - a
well-made output that passes review because review checks the surface. They are now one red

**exactly one beat per board is adversarial** - two red beats would have been a flag, not a
permission. Beat 10 is the Best Catch feeder.

**2. Word beat 1 is designated shared framing ("taught once"), so it carries the thesis for
both halves.** It absorbs PowerPoint's "it looks finished and says nothing" twice over: as
the closing strip on beat 1 (a tidy deck, *"a deck can look finished and say nothing"*) and
as the left-hand setup on beat 6, where the six tidy slides with the argument slide missing
is the *reason* to do structure first. Nothing was dropped; it was relocated to where it
does work.

**3. The sidebar is the same Microsoft 365 add-in family for both apps.** Taught once, on
two windows, in 20 seconds.

**4. Both briefs closed on a green demo beat.** One green beat (11) carries both payloads,
and the deck cut-away moved up to beat 6, where agreeing an outline in chat is the thing
being taught. Two badges, ~15 seconds each.

## Judgement calls worth recording

**1. Beat 10 is the one wide slot (1600, not 1200), and that is deliberate.** It carries
two failures *and* the catch that covers both, which is three columns' worth. The house
rule allows "an inherently wide beat to run a little wider, within reason". The first
attempt kept it at 1200 and it ran **250px taller than every other slot**, which is worse:
on a 16:10 laptop **height is what binds framing**, so a 1600 x 980 beat frames more
comfortably than a 1200 x 1230 one. **Do not solve this by making it taller.** The narration
tells the presenter to zoom out a notch for beat 10 and back in for beat 11.

**2. One worked example runs the whole board** - a generic packaging-supplier contract that
becomes a re-tender deck. The agreement is the document you edit (beats 1, 3, 4, 5) and the
case for re-tendering is the deck you build (beats 6, 7, 8, 11); beat 10 breaks both halves
of it. Pinned at the top of the build as `CLAUSE_BEFORE`, `CLAUSE_AFTER`, `LOST_CONDITION`,
`DEFINED_TERMS`, `DECISION`, `TOPIC_TO_CLAIM`, `GENERIC_HEADLINES` and `OUTLINE`.
**This is what makes it one video rather than two halves sharing a canvas - change the
example and beats 1, 3, 4, 5, 6, 7, 8, 10 and 11 all move together.**

**3. The board names the tools, never the tier.** "Word" and "PowerPoint" appear in the
board title, on beat 2's two windows and in the demo badges, and nowhere else - not one of
the eleven headings needs either word. This is Day 12's on-board / on-card line, and it is
what makes the two halves' **conflicting tier claims a card problem rather than a board
problem**: the module-2.9 Word script says the add-in is "Pro and up"; Day 12's card,
verified against the docs on 2026-09-14, says the add-in is **GA on Pro, Max, Team and
Enterprise** as part of Claude for Microsoft 365. The card carries the correction and the
board never had to know.

**4. Beat 5 is the fastest-dating line on the board, and the board says so.** "Strong at the
ends, thinner through the middle" is a claim about model behaviour on long inputs and it
will move. It earns its place because it changes what a person **types** - ask for the quote
and the page, not the summary - which is the same filter Day 3 and Day 4 apply to their
sources. Same treatment as Day 3's beat 15 and Day 11's beat 7: **re-check before
re-recording.**

**5. Beat 9 is scoped to judgement, not mechanics** - inherited from Day 12, where it was a
documented correction. "Design is not its strength" means it will not make **brand
decisions** for you. It demonstrably *does* respect a template you hand it (the docs say it
reads the slide master, layouts, fonts and colour scheme and aims to maintain template
compliance). So the right-hand column is worded as **decisions** ("which template to use",
"what the brand actually is"), never as objects ("the template"). Narration that drifts
into "it will not respect your template" puts the board and the card in contradiction.

**6. The red data caution sits on beat 11 rather than on the card.** The eleven-beat brief
has no slot for it, but this is a mandatory course for a regulated pharmacy and it is the
only on-screen data-handling rule a viewer sees. Same call Day 7, Day 10 and Day 12 each
made. The untrusted-file half rides with it because a deck template and an outside contract
are both files from outside, and beat 8 is the beat that tells people to feed outside files
in. **Do not quietly drop it.**

**7. The human gate on beat 9 is GREEN**, not the beat's teal and not Day 12's indigo.
**Green = the human pass is held constant across Day 10, Day 11 and Day 13**, and that
marker is safety-bearing, so it reads as the same rule here as it does there. It uses a
bespoke `human_gate()` rather than the kit's `person()`, which is sized for a crowd of tiny
figures and reads as a paper dart standing alone - the same call Day 10 and Day 12 made.

**8. Accents: the deck half does not re-run the prose half's sequence.** The prose half is
orange / violet / blue / teal / indigo; the deck half opens on **yellow**, a colour the
prose half never uses, so the pivot at beat 6 is visible from the colour alone before a word
is read. No accent repeats on adjacent beats.

**9. The three decks on beat 10 carry no jitter and one neutral grey** - a deliberate
exception to the house jitter rule, because the sameness *is* the point and a tilt or a
colour each would read as three real options. Day 10's beat 6 and Day 12's beat 6 both
record the identical exception. Do not "fix" any of them.

import re

body="## 0:00"+open(p).read().split("## 0:00",1)[1].split("## On-screen demo steps")[0]

for sec in re.split(r"\n## ", body):
    sec=sec.strip()

    txt=re.sub(r"`\[.*?\]`.*","",txt); txt=re.sub(r"(?m)^>.*$","",txt)

print(f"{tot:5d}  TOTAL = {tot/150*60/60:.2f} min spoken at 150 wpm")

## Constraints this board was built under

Verbatim from the commissioning brief, so a later editor can check the work against it.

- Thin `build*.py` doing `from excalidraw_kit import *`, defining scene + scaffold
  (`OX` / `WID` / `ACCENT` / `beat_head`) and calling `finish(out, max_w, total_w)`. Never
  hand-place elements in the `.excalidraw`. Never edit `excalidraw_kit.py`.
- Deterministic `random.seed` (here: `141214`) so re-runs are identical.
- Pan order documented in the module docstring.
- No Excalidraw frame elements - a literal frame is a hard `build_all.py` guard failure.
  No boxes, no connector spine, no inter-beat arrows, no colour-band washes, white
  background. Beats separated by whitespace only.
- Uniform slots ~1120 x 980 with `GAP = 800`; an inherently wide beat may run a little
  wider (here: beat 10 only). Pairs stacked vertically, grids near-square.
- All text in the hand font (fontFamily 1), roughness 1, lineHeight 1.4. Headings via the
  kit's `heading()`. No step-number circles, no highlighter sweeps, no sparkles. Body and
  explanation text stays ink or grey.
- Light rotation jitter on notes and chips (except beat 10's three decks - see above).
- Examples generic, not Phlo-specific; no patient or clinical specifics; fictional
  identifiers only. Product proper nouns exact. British English, hyphens not em dashes, no
  Oxford commas.
- One beat per board is adversarial, always red - here, beat 10.
