**Format:** one flowing Excalidraw board (`claude-powerpoint.excalidraw`) you pan across left-to-right and talk over, cutting to live Claude / live PowerPoint between beats · ~4:30 Loom · British English · invented, generic examples (no Phlo branding, no patient or clinical data) · current to today.

**How to use this pack**

## Section 2 - Narration script (~4:30)

Voice: warm, plain, a colleague showing you something useful - not a corporate voiceover. Times are a guide.

**[BEAT 1] 0:00 - 0:18 (Hook)**
"Ask Claude to make you a slide deck, and most people expect an outline - a list of what the slides *should* say. Claude can just build the deck: a real PowerPoint file, sitting there ready to open and present. That's the whole idea of this one. We met file-making briefly in the Artifacts video; here we go deep on slides. Four and a half minutes."

**[BEAT 2] 0:18 - 0:50 (What it is)**
"Here's what's actually happening. You ask in the chat, on the left, like always. Claude writes a genuine PowerPoint file by running code, and hands it to you on the right - you download it, or save it straight to Google Drive. It comes out as a real dot-pptx: it opens in PowerPoint, Google Slides or Keynote, like any file a colleague would send you. This is on every plan - web, desktop and mobile - up to thirty megabytes a file. No special software, no add-ons needed for this part."

**[BEAT 3] 0:50 - 1:10 (Start from what you have)**
"And you almost never start from a blank slide. The trick is to point Claude at raw material you already have - some rough notes, a Word report, a sheet of numbers, last week's meeting summary - and let it do the shaping into slides. You bring the substance; Claude brings the structure."

**[BEAT 4] 1:10 - 1:35 (Tell it the shape) → [CUT TO CLAUDE DESKTOP]**
"The more you say up front, the less you fix later. Worth saying: how many slides and who it's for; the tone - board-level, or a relaxed team catch-up; whether you want a summary slide and speaker notes; and that it should build charts from your numbers rather than just listing them. Things like 'build a ten-slide deck for the leadership review', or 'make it match our template and add speaker notes'. Let's actually do one."

**[CUT TO CLAUDE DESKTOP] 1:35 - 2:20 (Demo 1 - see Section 3.1)**
Paste some notes, ask for a deck, point at the file as it builds, then download it. Keep it moving.

**[BACK TO BOARD] [BEAT 5] 2:20 - 2:45 (Build from chat, or edit in PowerPoint)**
"Now - there are *two* places Claude can do this. What we just did was in the Claude chat: describe it, Claude builds the whole file from scratch. The second place is *inside* PowerPoint itself. There's a Claude sidebar - a Microsoft 365 add-in - that works on the deck you've already got open, editing it slide by slide. That one needs a Pro plan or above and the add-in installed. Same Claude, two front doors."

**[BEAT 6] 2:45 - 3:20 (It's a draft you steer) → [CUT TO POWERPOINT]**
"Either way, the first pass is rarely the last - and you don't redo it, you steer it by asking. 'Simplify the text on slide three.' 'Add a market-sizing section.' 'Turn these bullets into a process diagram.' 'Combine slides five and six.' It edits in place and keeps your template and branding, so it still looks like your deck. Let me show the sidebar doing exactly that."

**[CUT TO POWERPOINT] 3:20 - 3:50 (Demo 2 - see Section 3.2)**
In an open deck, use the Claude sidebar to edit one slide and turn some bullets into a chart. Accept the change, then undo to show it's reversible.

**[BACK TO BOARD] [BEAT 7] 3:50 - 4:05 (Decks worth handing over)**
"Which decks are worth handing over? Turning a report into slides - paste the document, get a deck. The weekly team update on a recurring template. A training deck that walks through a process step by step. And honestly, just a first draft to react to - it's far faster to fix a draft than to face a blank file."

**[BEAT 8] 4:05 - 4:25 (Before the deck leaves your hands)**
"Before a deck leaves your hands, a quick check. Make sure the facts and numbers are right; that nothing confidential is sitting in a deck you'll send; and that you've actually read it - because you own the final version. And one thing to avoid: don't open templates or decks you don't trust, because a booby-trapped file can quietly hijack the request."

**[BEAT 9] 4:25 - 4:40 (Your turn - notes into a deck)**
"Your turn. Take something you've already written - a project update, a process you explain a lot, last month's numbers - and ask Claude for a five-slide version. Open it in PowerPoint and change one thing, so it's properly yours. Claude turns raw material into a finished deck - you still hold the pen. Docs are linked below. See you in the next one."

## Section 3 - On-screen demo steps

Everything below is invented, generic data. No patient information, no Phlo specifics, at any point.

### 3.1 Demo 1 - notes to a deck, in the Claude chat (~45s)

1. In a new chat, paste a short block of plain notes (invent them on the spot - e.g. a fictional "Q3 customer-support review": volumes up 12%, three recurring issues, two proposed fixes, budget ask). Then type:
   > Turn these notes into a 10-slide PowerPoint deck for a leadership review. Add a title slide, a one-slide summary, a chart for the volume numbers, and speaker notes. Keep it clean.
2. Claude builds the file. Point at it: "that's a real file building, not a description of one."
3. Iterate once - type:
   > Make slide 3 punchier - fewer words - and add a closing 'next steps' slide.
   Show it produce an updated file.
4. **Download** the .pptx (or note the *Save to Google Drive* option). Say: "thirty-megabyte limit, every plan." Switch back to the board.

### 3.2 Demo 2 - the add-in, inside PowerPoint (~30s)

1. Open PowerPoint (web or desktop) on an existing short deck with your template applied. Open the **Claude** sidebar (the Microsoft 365 add-in).
2. On a busy slide, type into the sidebar:
   > Simplify the text on this slide, and turn the bullet list into a bar chart.
3. **Review** the proposed change, **Accept** it, then **Undo** to show it's reversible and you're always in control.
4. Mention: "Pro plan and up, and it respects the template that's already on the deck." Switch back to the board.

> Tip if a demo wobbles on camera: just describe the problem in plain English ("the chart used the wrong column") - re-runs are cheap, keep talking.

## Section 4 - Design rationale (why it looks the way it does)

- **House Style B**: one flowing, hand-drawn journey - no frames, white canvas, the Virgil hand font, lively colour-coded Excalidraw palette. Built by `build_powerpoint.py` (imports the shared `excalidraw_kit`); re-run it to regenerate the board.
- **Distinct from the file-type siblings**: this video's angle is *decks* - structure, templates/branding and turning bullets into native charts/diagrams. Excel (2.8) owns formulas and live data; Word (2.9) owns long-form and formatting. Kept separate so the three don't read as copy-paste.
- **Two real capabilities, not conflated**: (1) "Create and edit files with Claude" - the chat builds a genuine downloadable .pptx, all plans, web/desktop/mobile; (2) "Claude for PowerPoint" - the Microsoft 365 add-in sidebar, Pro and up. Beat 5 is the hinge that distinguishes them.
- **Safety framing kept honest**: the real, documented cautions - prompt-injection risk from untrusted files, and human review of regulated/client-facing output - map onto Phlo's "human owns the final" guardrail without inventing anything.
